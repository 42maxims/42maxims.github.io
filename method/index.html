How Moral Rank works

Moral Rank asks one question: how widely, independently and reliably has humanity arrived at this idea?

Each quote is weighted by how well it is verified (verified 1.0, probable 0.6, unverified 0) and how closely it expresses the maxim. Breadth matters more than volume: each independent tradition adds a lot, each era adds a good deal, while extra quotes and thinkers add less and less. A tradition that grew out of another already supporting the maxim (for example Christianity from Judaism) counts at half weight.

E = ln(1+Q) + √K + 3·T + 1.5·R, then normalised so the top maxim scores 100.

The score measures convergence, not truth or moral worth. We never rank traditions or thinkers, only ideas. Full details are in the spec.

Verification labels

Verified: wording checked against a primary text or a reputable translation. Probable: checked against a reliable secondary source only. Unverified: origin unknown; shown, but scores zero.

Translations of non-English texts are public-domain or credited translations. Where we make our own, they are AI-assisted and labelled as such.
