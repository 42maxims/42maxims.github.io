Seventeen-Article Constitution

604 (as dated in the Nihongi, 720)Early Japanese thought (Shōtoku)Medieval (600 – 1500)Prince ShōtokuSaying or principle

A set of seventeen moral precepts for officials, recorded in the Nihongi and credited to Prince Shōtoku. It opens by valuing harmony and draws on Confucian ideas of order and Buddhist reverence; it was guidance for conduct rather than a constitution in the modern sense.

Little is known for certain here; this background is thin and may be revised.
