Source

Mencius

4th century BCE (compiled by disciples, scholarly view)ConfucianismAncient (before 200 BCE)MenciusBook

An anthology of conversations and anecdotes of the philosopher Mencius, many of them with rulers of the Warring States period. It is one of the Confucian classics and sets out his moral and political philosophy of benevolent government.

  • “There were the old and wifeless, or widowers, the old and husbandless, or widows; the old and childless, or solitaries; and the young and fatherless, or orphans:—these four classes are the most destitute under heaven, and have none to whom they can tell [their wants], and king Wan, in the institution of his government with its benevolent action, made them the first objects of his regard.”
    — Mencius, Mencius I.ii.5 (1B.5), tr. James Legge, The Life and Works of Mencius (1875), p. 148; Legge's brackets kept
