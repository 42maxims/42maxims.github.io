Zhuangzi

c. 369–286 BCE (traditional)TaoismAncient (before 200 BCE)Person

Zhuang Zhou, known as Zhuangzi, was a Chinese thinker of the Warring States period and, with Laozi, the main early voice of Taoism. The book named after him mixes stories, dialogues and jokes; scholars usually credit him with the Inner Chapters and see the rest, including the story of his wife's death, as the work of later followers.

Little is known for certain here; this background is thin and may be revised.
